The supplier can load your materials, but who will unload them when the truck arrives? If you're arranging a delivery to an Edmonton jobsite without a suitable forklift, settle that question before arranging the transport. A truck arriving with the load doesn't automatically mean unloading has been arranged.

A MOFFETT is a truck-mounted forklift that travels with the delivery vehicle. For suitable loads and site conditions, it lets you discuss transport and offloading together. The next step is to check the materials, pickup arrangements and route from the truck to the intended drop point. Having no site forklift is the starting question, rather than confirmation that a particular delivery will work.

## What changes when the delivery brings a forklift?

MOFFETT is the equipment brand. You'll also hear this type of service called truck-mounted forklift delivery. Instead of relying entirely on equipment at the destination, the delivery vehicle carries a forklift that can be used for suitable offloading.

A Moffett-equipped delivery vehicle can also be a flatbed. The useful distinction is whether suitable unloading equipment and people are already arranged at the receiving site, or whether the forklift needs to travel with the delivery.

If the receiver already has the right equipment, discuss the cargo and handling arrangements with the carrier. [Flatbed transportation](/services/flatbed-transportation/) may fit that situation. Confirm who will unload, when they'll be available and whether their equipment suits the individual packages.

If there's no suitable equipment at the destination, ask about [MOFFETT delivery](/services/moffett-delivery/). The load, access and intended placement still need review. A forklift travelling with the truck doesn't make every load or location suitable. If you don't know which arrangement fits, explain what's available at each end and ask before making the plan.

## Check with the supplier and receiving site

You may need information from two different people. The supplier usually knows how the materials are packaged, how many pallets or bundles there are, their weights and dimensions, and when they'll be ready for collection. Ask what loading equipment is available at pickup too. Bringing a forklift for delivery offloading doesn't automatically settle the loading arrangements at the supplier.

The receiver knows the site entrance, receiving hours, access restrictions and where the materials are meant to go. Ask them to describe the full path from the truck area to that spot. An entrance that looks wide enough in one photo may lead to a tighter turn, parked equipment or a soft area further inside.

Total load weight and the weight of each lift answer different questions. A total helps describe the transport requirement. Individual pallet, bundle or crate weights help with the offloading discussion. If the supplier has only an estimate, identify it as an estimate. Leave unknown details open rather than filling them in with a guess.

Sending these details starts the suitability and availability discussion. It doesn't confirm acceptance of the load or book a delivery date. You don't need to hide an uncertainty to make an enquiry useful. Knowing that a lift weight or access measurement still needs checking helps everyone understand what remains to be settled.

## Take photos of the path to the drop point

A close-up of the materials is useful, but it doesn't explain how the delivery will reach the intended placement area. Wider views can help connect the load, the truck area and the receiving site.

Useful views include:

- The approach and entrance, including gates or overhead restrictions.
- The area where the truck would arrive and any nearby obstructions.
- The path between that area and the intended drop point.
- The placement area, including slopes or visibly soft and uneven ground.
- The packaged materials, with any available supplier dimensions and weights.

Photos supplement measurements and the suitability discussion. They don't establish lifting capacity, clearance or whether a surface is suitable. Describe restrictions that may be hard to see, and ask how to share photos when you get in touch. The [Moffett service page](/services/moffett-delivery/) also explains the load and site details to discuss.

## Report changes before the delivery

Sites change between the first enquiry and the requested delivery day. An area that was clear may become storage space. Parked equipment, an excavation or other construction work can affect the approach or placement area. Receiving contacts and hours can change too.

Tell the carrier when the arrangements change. Report snow, ice, thawed ground or other conditions that may affect access, rather than assuming the original photos still describe the site. A previous discussion doesn't establish that changed conditions will be suitable. Updated information gives you a chance to revisit the plan before the truck arrives.
