# Flatbed keyword evidence

Research date: 2026-11-10. Target market: Edmonton, Alberta. This memo supports the November keyword report. It does not authorize website edits or establish search demand measurements.

## Recommendation

Assign the flatbed, flat deck and flatdeck commercial terms to the existing `/services/flatbed-transportation/` page. Use `flatbed transport Edmonton` as the proposed primary phrase. Keep `flatbed trucking Edmonton`, `flat deck delivery Edmonton` and `flatdeck transportation Edmonton` as supporting variants on that same page.

This selection is editorial judgment based on service fit and current provider language. It is not a claim that the primary phrase has the highest search volume, lowest competition or best conversion rate. No measured comparison between the spellings was available.

Keep `flatbed transportation Alberta` and `flatdeck trucking Alberta` as monitored research candidates. Alberta relevance does not verify Iron Fist's service coverage. Do not publish a province-wide promise or create location pages without owner-confirmed service facts and a useful reason for each page.

## Method and limits

Used the available web search and page-text tools without a paid subscription or private account. Search terms explicitly included Edmonton or Alberta. The tool did not expose an Edmonton search location, a local Google results page, Google Maps positions, or a repeatable organic ranking export. Returned ordering is not reported as Google ranking.

Three queries were submitted together in each search batch. The tool returned combined results, so this record attributes observations to the batch rather than claiming a precise result set for each query. Exact-phrase queries were a terminology cross-check, not a demand measurement. They returned substantial irrelevant material and should not determine primary targets by themselves.

No Keyword Planner, Search Console or Trends dataset was obtained in this subtask. Monthly search volume, Google Ads advertiser competition, organic keyword difficulty, CPC, impressions, clicks and actual ranking positions are unavailable. Missing values are not zero. This memo does not claim a check of those products' account access.

No rendered competitor audit, test enquiry, traffic estimate, backlink review or independent operational verification was performed. A page read supports its visible language and customer questions, not proof of its promises.

## Sources and observed intent

### Delivery Tech

Source: [Flat decks](https://deliverytech.ca/equipment/flat-decks/). Search discovery and full available page-text read on 2026-11-10.

The page describes open trailers, long freight, side loading, crane or forklift handling, cargo securement and weather protection. It asks customers to contact the business about a shipment. The result title connects flat deck deliveries with Edmonton. This supports commercial delivery intent and the usefulness of explaining handling arrangements. Its securement and tarping statements are Delivery Tech's terms, not Iron Fist's.

Editorial implication: use a delivery synonym naturally, while keeping load dimensions and equipment at both ends central to the enquiry. Do not equate a flat deck with a forklift supplied for offloading.

### Sema Trucking

Source: [Alberta hotshot and flatbed transportation](https://sematrucking.ca/). Search discovery and full available page-text read on 2026-11-10.

The Edmonton-based provider uses flatbed transportation wording and discusses construction materials and industrial cargo. It combines general freight, hotshot, short-haul, long-haul and specialist load offers. A quote form asks for origin and destination. This supports commercial carrier selection and route-specific enquiry intent. Its fleet, coverage and handling claims do not establish Iron Fist capabilities.

Editorial implication: cargo examples can clarify the use case, but broad provincial queries also expose customers to specialist services. Iron Fist should explain its actual enquiry scope rather than adopt those providers' wider offers.

### Rene Transport

Source: [Truckload service](https://www.renetransport.com/truckload). Search discovery and full available page-text read on 2026-11-10.

The truckload page places flat deck alongside other trailer types and describes lumber, pipe and equipment. It offers a quote/contact route. This supports a commercial freight intent and shows that trailer choice depends on cargo. Its equipment dimensions and national operations belong to Rene.

Editorial implication: the flatbed page should answer whether the load and handling arrangement fit, then route the visitor to an enquiry. Generic truckload and tractor-service language can cover several different services, so Iron Fist's distinctions need to remain explicit.

## Exclusions and page separation

Do not deliberately target trailer rental, trailers for sale, towing, driver jobs, owner-operator recruitment, load-board listings or training. Rental and recruitment material appeared among the retrieved results, and these do not match the customer task for this service page.

Do not target heavy haul, oversize permits, lowboy, step deck, Super B, hotshot, same-day delivery, emergency delivery or cross-border trucking as Iron Fist offers without confirmation. Several providers combine these with flatbed transport. That association does not make them supported Iron Fist services.

Keep the homepage focused on the business and its overall construction/industrial transport offering. The services overview should help the visitor choose between the three services. Detailed flatbed booking intent belongs to the flatbed page. Forklift-assisted delivery and site offloading intent belongs to the Moffett page. Customer-supplied trailer and tractor/driver intent belongs to the power-only page.

No separate flatdeck page or spelling-specific page is needed. Preserve the current flatbed URL. The current page already explains the synonym and covers cargo dimensions, loading/unloading, packaging, restrictions and weather protection. Research supports refining its wording later, not a broad rewrite or a claim that those topics are missing.

The planned November Moffett article can link to this page when explaining what changes if suitable unloading equipment is already arranged. It should not become a second flatbed booking page or imply that every flatbed load can use the mounted forklift.

## Business facts still required before stronger claims

Equipment dimensions and capacity, route coverage, specialist load acceptance, tarping provision, operating hours, lead times and prices remain unconfirmed in the source research. Ask about these only when a planned factual statement needs them. Their absence does not block this keyword map or a qualified enquiry-led page.
