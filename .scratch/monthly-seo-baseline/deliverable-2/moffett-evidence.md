## Article decision

Recommended working title: **No forklift on site? Planning a Moffett delivery in Edmonton**.

The article would answer how a contractor or supplier should arrange material delivery when the receiving site has no suitable unloading equipment. Start with the delivery decision, then provide a usable enquiry template that joins supplier and receiver information. This is an editorial recommendation from service fit and observed provider questions. It is not a claim that the exact title is a high-volume keyword.

Suggested informational phrase cluster: `what is Moffett delivery`, `delivery without a forklift on site`, `Moffett delivery requirements`, and `how to prepare for Moffett delivery`. These are candidate phrases for natural copy. The comparison/preparation concepts were searched above; no exact-phrase monthly counts were obtained.

The existing commercial page already explains individual lift weights, packaging, access, surfaces, overhead restrictions, intended placement, pickup equipment and changed site conditions. A longer version of that same checklist adds little. Keep the service page's short enquiry checklist; make the article useful through a delivery-method decision and a copyable information packet.

Suggested outline:

1. Explain the receiving-site problem and briefly define a truck-mounted Moffett forklift.
2. Distinguish transport-only flatbed delivery from flatbed delivery with a truck-mounted forklift. A Moffett-equipped vehicle can also be a flatbed, so do not present them as mutually exclusive vehicle types.
3. Ask what equipment the supplier has at pickup and what is available at delivery. Bringing a forklift does not automatically settle both ends.
4. Provide a copyable enquiry template with pickup/delivery addresses, date, supplier/receiver contacts, packaging, item count, total and per-lift weights, dimensions, available pickup equipment and intended placement.
5. Add a short photo/measurement checklist for approach, entrance, truck area, forklift path and drop point. Photos do not replace dimensions or suitability review.
6. Include one clearly labelled hypothetical enquiry example. No invented completed job, customer, location or outcome.
7. Briefly mention changed ground or weather conditions, including snow or ice when relevant. Ask the reader to report current conditions rather than promising winter access.
8. Link to the Moffett service page to discuss suitability and availability. Link to flatbed only where the reader already has unloading arrangements.

The earlier generic preparation angle remains viable only if built around these practical additions. A winter-only article has weaker support in this research: an Alberta supplier answers winter questions, but no Edmonton seasonal demand or Iron Fist winter operating terms were measured. Keep winter as a limited section unless the owner supplies suitable experience and photos.

## Factual and photo inputs

Existing repository images `src/assets/images/gallery/trucks/moffetiandtruck.webp` and `moffeti.webp` are already used on the service page. Their visible forklift/trailer scenes can illustrate equipment, with literal alt text. No new image or file modification was made. Before drafting a case study or specifying operational limits, obtain owner-confirmed facts. The current research supports proceeding with general enquiry guidance without those claims. Missing factual inputs include actual equipment model/capacity, clearance and surface limits, seasonal policies, rates, lead times and precise coverage. Do not infer them from competitors, manufacturer product ranges, old site snippets or photographs.
